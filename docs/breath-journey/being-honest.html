Three · being honest about it

What breathwork does in randomised trials, and how much weight each claim on this page can carry.

Part three · being honest about it

What it measurably does

2023

Somebody gathered the randomised trials of breathwork and pooled them. The effect is real, it is small-to-medium, and the authors went out of their way to warn against the version of their own result that was about to get quoted.

The research Fincham, Strauss, Montero-Marin & Cavanagh, 20231. From 1325 initial results they took randomised controlled trials only: 12 trials and 785 adults for stress, 20 for anxiety, 18 for low mood.

The best evidence on the question, and the authors say most of the trials were at moderate risk of bias — which for a breathing intervention is close to unavoidable, since nobody can be blinded to how they are breathing. Their own closing sentence asks for better research “to avoid a miscalibration between hype and evidence.”

Only stress has a published confidence interval, so only stress gets a bar. The other two are drawn as bare points, because a made-up interval would be a worse figure than an incomplete one.

Stress came out at a standardised effect of −0.35, somewhere between −0.55 and −0.14. Anxiety came out at −0.32 and low mood at −0.40, both better than the control conditions — though the abstract publishes no interval for either, which is why the figure draws them as bare points.

In plain terms: this works, and it works about as well as a decent self-help intervention works. It is not nothing and it is not transformative. If somebody leaves your room breathing more slowly than they arrived, you have done something worth doing, and you have not rewired them.

Two limits worth carrying. Every one of these outcomes is self-reported — how people say they feel, which is the easiest thing in the field to shift and also the thing that matters most to them. And “breathwork” pools a lot of quite different practices, which is part of why the trials disagree with each other.

For your hands The evidence supports offering this warmly and describing it modestly. “This reliably helps people feel less stressed, by a useful amount” is well within what the trials show. Anything about states, systems or rewiring is not.

How much each of these can carry

Now

Every stop on this page rests on a source, and the sources are not equally good. Flattening that would undo most of the point of writing it down.

The research All of it, weighed against each other. The placement below is our judgement, not a measurement, and the caption says so — what it is for is the ordering.

The bottom entry has no source at all, which is the honest way to record it. “Breathing technique changes long-run health” is the claim that sells the workshops, and we could not find trials that establish it. Its absence is a finding.

A judgement, not a measurement. The value is the gradient: the best-supported claims are the ones you can check on your own wrist, and the one with the most riding on it sits at the bottom with nothing under it.

Notice the shape. The top of this list is made of things that are directly observable — your heart rate follows your breath; the swing is largest around six a minute. They need no trial because you can watch them happen.

The bottom is where the money is. Breathing technique changes long-run health is the claim that fills rooms, and there is no adequate evidence for it — not evidence against it, which is a different thing, but an absence where the trials would be. That is worth saying out loud precisely because everything above it is so solid. A page that establishes four real things is not licensed to wave the fifth through.

For your hands The strongest thing you can offer is also the most checkable: a rate, a reason, and something the person can verify on their own pulse. It is a smaller claim than the one they may have heard, and it has the advantage of being true.
