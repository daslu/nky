Touch is not one sense

Skin carries at least two separate systems, and they are not sending the same message. One tells you what you are touching. The other answers mainly to being touched affectionately. It was found by accident, confirmed by a man who had lost the ordinary sense of touch, and it fires hardest at one particular speed.

This is a reading journey through the research on touch — the sense, not the technique — in a series of short stops and about half an hour. Every figure can be operated. Every claim names its source before you meet it, and says how much that source can carry.

What follows is what we found reading our way through it: what each system responds to, what touch does to pain and to relationships, and which of the popular claims we could not get to stand up.

Who it is for

Anyone whose work involves putting hands on other people: massage therapists, bodyworkers, physiotherapists, osteopaths, nurses, midwives, somatic and trauma practitioners. No science background is assumed — we did not begin with much of one either.

Every claim points to a named paper, cited at the top of its section and listed at the foot of the page it appears on, so you can go and check where we have read it wrong. And where researchers disagree, which here they do a good deal, we have tried to say so rather than pick a side.

What is here

One · the territory. Two roads, the receptors they arrive on, and how finely skin can actually resolve what it feels.

Two · the second system. How the slow road was found, the patient who proved it carries an experience, and the two things it is tuned to.

Three · what touch does. Pain, the map of who may touch you where, and an honest sorting of how sure anyone can be about any of it.

Four · being honest about it. Four things the field has had to take back, and why the fibre is not the feeling.

What we carry back into the room. What we came away with, where to go next, and every source in the order the journey met them.

Nothing here is medical advice, and nothing here is a technique manual. It is a record of what two readers found in the literature, assembled in 2026 from the literature as it stood — and it cannot tell you what the person in front of you wants.

Before you start If you take one thing from this: the same touch is not the same experience. Most of what follows is the two systems and what each of them answers to — slow, warm, moving contact on hairy skin. Part four is about why that turns out to be the smaller half.
