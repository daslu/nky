What we would carry back into the room

The end of it

What we came away with. Not instructions — a record of what the reading suggested, in the order the page found them.

The research Everything cited on the previous pages, weighed as part four weighs it. Nothing new is introduced here.

The same judgement as the previous page, repeated because it is the thing to leave with.

  • Hurt and harm are different quantities. The amount something hurts is not a reading of how damaged it is, and the two can move independently in both directions.

  • A worn-looking scan is what most backs of that age look like. At sixty, 88% of people with no pain at all have disc degeneration. That does not make a finding meaningless; it makes it uninformative on its own.

  • What a sensation is believed to mean is part of what is felt. A −20 °C rod under a red light was rated five and a half points hotter than the same rod under a blue one.

  • Signals are turned up and down before they arrive. That is what a hand on a banged shin is doing, and it is a real mechanism rather than a distraction.

  • Long-standing pain can be a settings change rather than more damage. The gain goes up. It is also not something anyone can diagnose from a treatment room.

  • The words are part of the treatment. Your pelvis is out is an input, not a description. So is being unhurried, and so is being believed.

  • Explaining pain does something, and it is not large. Twelve trials, and the effect on pain intensity is small enough that no effect is still on the table.

  • The most repeated story is usually the weakest evidence. The nail through the boot is one paragraph in a magazine column and it travels further than any of the trials.

  • Almost nobody in front of you has something rare. Back pain is the most ordinary presentation on earth, and the base rate for nothing sinister is very high.

For your hands If a client leaves with one sentence, we would want it to be that their back is more ordinary than the report makes it sound, and that hurting is not the same as being damaged. Both of those are true, and both are the kind of true you can say without promising anything.
